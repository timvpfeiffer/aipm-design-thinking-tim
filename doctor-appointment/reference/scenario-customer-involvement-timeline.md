# Scenario: Real Customers Involved Throughout the Process

**This is an illustrative scenario, not real data.** The participants, quotes and numbers are invented to show how customer involvement could work. They are **simulated** and must never be copied into an evidence log as real findings.

**Project:** Helping working adults book a non-urgent GP appointment
**Team:** 1 Product Manager (PM), 1 designer, 1 developer
**Duration:** 4 weeks, with Day 1 as the start

## Overview

```
Week 1                    Week 2                  Week 3                Week 4
Empathise ──► Define ──►  Ideate ──► Prototype ──► Test ──────────────► Iterate / Decide
   ▲  (A)        ▲ (B)                    ▲ (C)       ▲ (D)  ▲ (E)           ▲ (F)
   customers     customers               customers   customers customers    customers
```

Customer touchpoints: **A** interviews, **B** frame check, **C** idea reaction, **D** round 1 test, **E** round 2 test, **F** follow-up.

## Timeline

| When | Mode | Team activity | Customer involvement | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Day 1** | Empathise | Journey map draft, with every claim marked "Assumption" | None yet. This is the hypothesis. | Draft journey map |
| **Day 2–3** | Empathise | Recruit 5 working adults (25–40) who booked or avoided a GP visit in the last year | **(A) 5 interviews, 30 min each.** Ask: "Tell me about the last time you tried to book." Past behaviour only, no ideas shown. | Direct evidence: quotes and what people did |
| **Day 4** | Empathise | Read 40 public reviews of local practices | **Reviews** as behavioural evidence. Count how often "can't get through" appears. | Frequency count |
| **Day 5** | Define | Cluster notes, update the journey map, relabel each cell (direct, behavioural, interpretation, assumption) | None. Synthesis is the team's job. | Updated journey map, clusters |
| **Day 6** | Define | Write POV, problem statement, HMW questions | **(B) 2 of the 5 interviewees** get a 10-min call: "Does this describe your situation?" | Confirmed or corrected problem frame |
| **Day 8** | Ideate | 30-minute brainstorm, then score ideas | None during brainstorming. Judgement is deferred and the team generates ideas alone. | Idea list, scores |
| **Day 9** | Ideate | Pick the idea with the highest learning value | **(C) 3 customers** see one-line idea descriptions and are asked which they would try and why. This is a weak signal, so the team treats it only as a hint. | Shortlist |
| **Day 10–11** | Prototype | Build a paper flow of the top idea | None. | Paper prototype, test plan |
| **Day 12** | Test | **Round 1** | **(D) 5 new customers, 15 min each.** Neutral task, "What would you do next?", observation only. | Observation / interpretation / decision table |
| **Day 13** | Iterate | Synthesize round 1 and change the prototype | None. | Revised prototype |
| **Day 15–16** | Test | **Round 2** | **(E) 5 new customers** on the revised version. | Updated table, confidence per assumption |
| **Day 17–18** | Iterate | Decide: continue, change course or stop | None. The decision is the team's, based on evidence. | Decision record |
| **Day 20** | Handover | Write user stories with the evidence attached, hand over to the Scrum backlog | **(F) 2–3 participants** from earlier rounds get a thank-you and a short preview ("Did we capture this correctly?"). | Backlog items with evidence links |

## What the customers did at each point, and why

| Touchpoint | What customers do | Why then | What the team must not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A: Interviews** | Describe their last real booking | Replaces the assumptions on the journey map | Mention the callback idea or ask "Would you use...?" |
| **B: Frame check** | React to the POV and problem statement | Catches a wrong frame before ideas are built on it | Use it as a vote on features |
| **C: Idea reaction** | Say which idea they'd try | Cheap, early signal | Treat it as proof. People say yes to be polite. |
| **D: Round 1** | Attempt a task on the paper prototype | Tests real behaviour, not opinions | Explain the solution or say "Do you like it?" |
| **E: Round 2** | Same task on the revised version | Checks whether the change helped | Test with the same 5 people (they now know the answer) |
| **F: Follow-up** | Confirm the summary | Keeps trust and checks interpretation | Ask for new requirements |

## Invented example of the loop working (illustration only)

1. **Interviews (Day 2–3):** 4 of 5 say they gave up after hold times. One says she calls at 07:30 and has no problem.
   *Interpretation:* The hold-time hypothesis is partly supported. A second group (early callers) exists.
2. **Frame check (Day 6):** 2 of 2 agree with the POV, but one adds, "I also don't know if my issue is worth a visit."
   *Decision:* Add the "is it serious enough?" cluster to the problem frame.
3. **Round 1 (Day 12):** 3 of 5 find the callback option. 2 ask, "Will they really call me?"
   *Interpretation:* Trust matters as much as convenience. *Decision:* Add a reassurance line to the confirmation card.
4. **Round 2 (Day 15–16):** 5 of 5 find the option, and only 1 asks about reliability.
   *Decision:* Continue. The sample is small, so confidence stays "moderate".

## Where the loop sends the team back

| Finding | Goes back to |
|---|---|
| Customers do not mention hold time at all | Empathise and Define: the problem frame is wrong |
| Customers like the idea but fail the task | Prototype: the flow is unclear |
| Customers trust the idea only with a human voice | Ideate: try another idea |
| Customers say "Fine" but nothing changes in behaviour | Test: ask for a task, not an opinion |

## Takeaways

- Customers appear at **six points**, not only at the end.
- Early customer contact (A, B) shapes the problem. Later contact (D, E) shapes the solution.
- The team synthesizes and decides. Customers provide evidence, not requirements.
- New participants are used in each round, so earlier learning doesn't bias the results.
- Every step produces a labelled output, so evidence stays separate from interpretation.
